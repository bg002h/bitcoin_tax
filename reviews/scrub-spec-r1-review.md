### [IMPORTANT] (buildable) §3.2's `malformed → malformed` rule is ambiguous, and its most natural reading passes the filer's REAL malformed SSN/EIN straight into the file the command authorizes as "safe to hand to a stranger."

**Location:** design/SPEC_income_scrub.md §3.2 (lines 102-109), esp. "A non-canonicalizable EIN must remain non-canonicalizable"

**Failure:** §3.2 spells out `valid → synthetic-valid` but writes the malformed leg as bare `malformed → malformed` with no "synthetic" qualifier, and its one worked example says a non-canonicalizable EIN "must REMAIN non-canonicalizable" — i.e. keep it. r1's own fix wording says the same thing three times ("leave a non-canonicalizable EIN un-replaced", "leave `String::new()`", scrub-r1-workflow.md:52/318). The asymmetry with the explicitly-synthetic valid leg reads as deliberate. Implemented that way: a filer who typo'd an SSN to eight digits (`Ssn::canonical`, packet.rs:58-73, rejects it) or an EIN to eight digits (`canonical_ein`, return_1040.rs:681-686, rejects it) — which is exactly the population the command targets, because that malformedness is what makes the return refuse — receives a "scrubbed" file carrying 8 of the 9 real digits of their own SSN and of their employer's EIN, next to every dollar figure, both parents' DOBs and the filing status. That is a disclosure in the direction r1's brief ranks worst, under the command's own safety authorization. r1's verifier flagged the risk once in passing (scrub-r1-workflow.md:269, "without passing 8 of the 9 real digits through into a file marked safe to share") and the spec did not carry it forward. Aggravated by §7: the exhaustive disclosure test that would catch it is step 5, two steps AFTER §3.2 lands at step 3.

**Fix:** State it as `malformed → SYNTHETIC-malformed, never the original value`, and add one governing sentence: no original identity value is ever emitted, in any class. Per class: SSN `Missing → ""`, `WrongLength(n) → a synthetic n-digit string`, `NotDigits(c) → a synthetic containing a non-digit`; EIN non-canonicalizable → a fixed synthetic non-canonical token (e.g. `"00-000000"`, eight digits). Move the exhaustive disclosure test (§7 step 5) to precede step 3 so the guard exists before the change that most needs it.
