EXECUTABLE AS WRITTEN: NO — The spine (transcribe, two oracles, type-level per-year shape) is sound, but five omissions each let a green suite print a wrong figure on a signed return: the TY2025 SALT limitation is not a scalar, `std_deduction`'s named source is superseded, Schedule 1-A's MAGI add-backs and its married-filing-jointly eligibility bar are absent from the spec entirely, and §6's part-by-part reconciliation is unachievable for Part IV against OpenTaxSolver 2025 as shipped. The spec also carries D-1's central justification — "both oracles implement it" — as a fact when R-3 concedes it was never read; it is wrong.

1. **Critical** — §5.1 treats `salt_cap` as a scalar to be re-read, but OBBBA's TY2025 §164(b)(6) is a $40,000 cap ($20,000 MFS), *reduced by 30% of MAGI over $500,000 ($250,000 MFS), floored at $10,000 ($5,000 MFS)* — a rule `FullReturnParams.salt_cap: Usd` (`crates/btctax-core/src/tax/tables.rs:326`, consumed at `return_1040.rs:322-327` and `printed.rs:1145` as a bare `min`) cannot express — **an itemizing filer with a large realized gain at MAGI $600,000 and $40,000 of state tax deducts $40,000 instead of $10,000; the only harness that exercises SALT is the golden corpus (`scripts/oracle/corpus.py:98-99,413-414`, whose cells are literally built around "capped to $10k"), and §8 Q2 leaves extending it to TY2025 *open*, so nothing in §6's six requirements runs SALT at 2025 constants** — replace the `salt_cap` bullet with a named sub-structure (cap / phase-down rate / MAGI threshold / floor, each per-status) and add "the TY2025 golden corpus must include an itemizing household above the §164(b)(6) phase-down threshold" to §6 as a numbered requirement, not an open question. Corroboration: taxcalc 6.7.2 `ID_AllTaxes_c` 2025 = [40000, 40000, 20000, 40000, 40000], `_po_rate` = 0.3, `_po_floor` = [10000, 10000, 5000, …].

2. **Critical** — §2 names Rev. Proc. 2024-40 as the constants source and §5.1 flags only `salt_cap` as OBBBA-changed, but OBBBA §70102 also superseded the TY2025 **standard deduction** (15,750 / 31,500 / 23,625 / 15,750 MFS vs. the Rev. Proc.'s 15,000 / 30,000 / 22,500) — **an implementer following §5.1 literally types the Rev. Proc. figures and every single filer's taxable income is $750 too high (MFJ $1,500, HoH $1,125); the existing constant test is `ty2025_single_ordinary_brackets_match_rev_proc_2024_40`, which covers brackets only, and no TY2025 golden exists to contradict it** — state a per-field source rule in §5.1 ("for each field, the later of Rev. Proc. 2024-40 and Pub. L. 119-21 controls; `std_deduction` and `salt_cap` are OBBBA") and require a test asserting the four TY2025 `std_deduction` values against the statute. Corroboration: taxcalc `STD` 2025 = [15750, 31500, 15750, 23625, 31500].

3. **Critical** — §5.2's enumerated "New input surface" omits Schedule 1-A **Part I lines 2a–2d** (excluded Puerto Rico income, Form 2555 lines 45 and 50, Form 4563 line 15), which are the difference between AGI and the MAGI that drives *all four* phase-outs — **a 68-year-old filer with excluded foreign earned income gets MAGI = AGI, so line 33's excess is understated and Part V pays out up to $6,000 too much on a part btctax computes without asking anyone; Tax-Calculator cannot witness it (its `MiscDed` docstring: "those inputs are not modeled in Tax-Calculator records, so `c00100` … is used directly as the MAGI proxy"), so a one-oracle reconciliation looks clean** — add lines 2a–2d to §5.2's input list explicitly and extend D-5 to name them, so that "every Schedule 1-A input is `Option`" has a surface that actually includes MAGI.

4. **Critical** — the spec never mentions filing-status eligibility, but Schedule 1-A Parts II, III and V each print *"If married, you must file jointly to claim this deduction"* — **btctax computes Part V from `date_of_birth` with no filer question (§5.2), so a 65+ MFS filer is handed a $6,000 deduction the form bars, and MFS is precisely the population §5.4 exists to build vectors for; §6 requirement 3 dimensions the census by status only for *AMT-owing* vectors, so no Schedule 1-A × MFS case is required to exist** — add to §5.2: "Parts II, III and V are zero for MFS by the form's own Caution; Part IV carries no such Caution and its MFS treatment is adjudicated against the instructions," plus a §6 requirement that Schedule 1-A reconcile across all five filing statuses. Note the oracles already split here: OTS bars *all four* parts for MFS (`taxsolve_US_1040_2025.c:1824`, `if (status != MARRIED_FILING_SEPARAT)`) while taxcalc allows Part IV for MARS=3, citing the form.

5. **Critical** — §6 requirement 4 ("reconciles against both engines part by part, including each phase-out's knee and its cap") is unachievable for **Part IV**: OTS 2025 multiplies line 28 by **300.0** where the form says **$200** (`taxsolve_US_1040_2025.c:1893`; taxcalc's `AutoLoanInterestDed_po_rate_per_step` = 0.2 confirms the form), **truncates** line 28 where the form says "increase to the next higher whole number", and prints the result under the label `S1A_20`, so line 29 is never emitted at all — **at MAGI $120,000 with $10,000 of qualified interest the form gives $6,000 and OTS gives $4,000, and since §6's "computed disqualification" escape hatch exists only in requirement 2 (scoped to `verify_f6251.py`), the cheapest route to green is to declare Part IV taxcalc-only — shipping a part on one oracle, which is exactly how the AMT MFS region became unwitnessed** — extend requirement 2's computed-disqualification rule to cover Schedule 1-A explicitly and record OTS 2025's three Part IV defects in §9/R-3 as *named, sized* disqualifications, so the escape is a stated one rather than an improvised one.

6. **Important** — D-4 fences per-year shape around Form 6251 only, but the **Form 1040 itself** changed shape for 2025 (11a/11b, 12e, 13a, **13b**, and line 14 = "Add lines 12e, 13a, and 13b"), and btctax's `AbsoluteReturn` is transcribed in 2024 numbering — `line14` is documented as "L12 + L13" and `ti_before_qbi: agi - deduction` (`return_1040.rs:897,1365`; `qbi.rs:42` "= 1040 AGI − L12") — **so D-3's "line 1a = Form 1040 line 14 − Sch 1-A line 37" wired to the existing `line14` omits 13b entirely, and Form 8995 line 11 keeps overstating taxable-income-before-QBI by the whole Schedule 1-A total, overstating the §199A deduction and firing the `qbi_over_threshold` refuse too late — and the fixture has no QBI vector at all** — add Form 1040 (and the §5.1 QBI ordering `11b − 12e − 13b`) to §5's scope and to D-4's coverage, so D-4 fences the form that feeds 6251 rather than only 6251.

7. **Important** — §6 requirement 4 names no harness and §5.3's seam list contains none for Schedule 1-A: `verify_f6251.py` is the Form 6251 harness, and `gen_goldens.py` hardcodes `FLPDYR: 2024` / `start_year=2024` and maps `"MARS": 2 if filing_status == "Married/Joint" else 1` — **HoH, MFS and QSS all reach taxcalc as Single, so the MFJ-doubled caps/thresholds and the MFS bar are unreachable in the corpus harness; separately, OTS's 2025 `US_1040` parser accepts an input file with no `S1A_2a` block (it falls through to `A1`, `taxsolve_US_1040_2025.c:2375-2391`), in which case `sched_1A()` is never called and L13b is silently 0 with no error — a template seam that reconciles perfectly on every household whose Schedule 1-A happens to be zero** — name the harness and add both seams (a real `MARS` map + threading the year through `gen_goldens.py`; the `S1A_*` template block in the OTS driver) to §5.3.

8. **Important** — §2's authority table archives `f1040s1a--2025.pdf` but **no Schedule 1-A instructions**, while the form defers to them in at least five places that change the number (line 4a's "see the instructions if Form W-2, box 5 is more than $176,100", line 4c's multi-employer/multi-occupation branch, line 5's multi-trade-or-business branch, line 22's ">two VINs", line 36a's "valid social security number (see instructions)") and defers occupation eligibility to IRS.gov/TippedOccupations, which is not a document at all — **the transcription rule produces doc comments that read "see the instructions" and an implementer resolves each branch by guessing, most cheaply as "the simple case"; a conformance test comparing doc comments to the *form* text passes on every one of them** — add the Schedule 1-A instructions (and Rev. Proc. 2024-40 / the OBBBA text) to §2's hashed table, and state in §5.2 that every "see instructions" branch is either transcribed or refuses.

9. **Important** — Tax-Calculator applies the stepped phase-out only when its `exact` input is 1 and otherwise uses a smoothed linear reduction (`calcfunctions.MiscDed`), and neither `gen_goldens.py` nor `verify_f6251.py` sets it — **§6 requirement 4's "each phase-out's knee" will diverge from btctax by up to $100 (Parts II/III) or $200 (Part IV) at every MAGI that is not a multiple of $1,000, and the fastest fix is a hand-written excuse that then also masks a genuine btctax rounding error — R-2's exact failure mode** — require `exact = 1` in the Schedule 1-A leg in §5.3, and note that `exact` co-governs `ChildDepTaxCredit` / `EducationTaxCredit` / `F2441` / `CTC_new`, so it must be set in a TY2025-scoped row builder rather than a shared one (this is the one real TY2024-contamination path I found: a shared Records column, not a shared constant).

10. **Minor** — §5.2 says Part V "is computable from `date_of_birth`", and the age test is exact (`is_aged`'s "born on or before January 1 of year−64" is the form's "born before January 2, 1961"), but line 36a also conditions on *"you have a valid social security number (see instructions)"*, which DOB does not answer and btctax cannot verify (an ITIN or an SSN marked not-valid-for-employment fails it) — **btctax pays a $6,000 deduction to an ITIN filer and both oracles agree with it, because taxcalc lists valid-SSN among its explicitly unmodeled conditions and OTS never asks** — state in §5.2 that Part V is DOB-driven *plus* an SSN-validity predicate that must be asked under D-5, since this is the residual half of what "the Part V per-spouse split" is actually collecting.
