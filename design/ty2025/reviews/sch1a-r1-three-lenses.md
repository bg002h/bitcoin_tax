### 1. Critical — [executable]

**Claim.** Part V's death-of-a-taxpayer rule is filed as an *open question* (§7.1) even though the spec's own archived authority states it as a rule with a worked example, and the repo has no date-of-death input — so btctax silently answers the eligibility question for the filer.

**Scenario.** MFJ; spouse born 1960-02-14, died 2025-02-12. `i1040gi--2025.pdf` (Part V, "Death of a taxpayer in 2025"): "If a taxpayer was born before January 2, 1961, but died in 2025 before reaching age 65, then the taxpayer doesn't qualify … A person is considered to reach age 65 on the day before the person's 65th birthday", with the exact worked example (died Feb 13 qualifies, Feb 12 does not). `is_aged(dob, 2025)` (return_1040.rs:42) returns true on the DOB alone, `grep date_of_death` over crates/ is empty, and §4.1 collects Part V from "`date_of_birth` … plus the valid-SSN predicate" only. Schedule 1-A line 36b is therefore filled with $6,000 (up to $12,000 on the two-senior case §5.5 requires) that the filer is not entitled to. Neither oracle models a death date, so the two-oracle sweep reconciles on the wrong figure and §5 goes green.

**Fix.** Move §7.1 from open question to a binding decision citing the archived instruction text; add a per-person `date_of_death: Option<Date>` (D-5 semantics, `None` refuses when Part V is claimed) to §4.1; and add a §5 item pinning both sides of the instructions' Feb-12/Feb-13 example as a KAT, mutation-verified.

**Evidence.** SPEC_schedule_1a.md:159-161 "Does Part V's death rule reach us? … does it collect a date of death?" vs i1040gi--2025.pdf p.110 "Death of a taxpayer in 2025…"; crates/btctax-core/src/tax/return_1040.rs:42-48 (`is_aged` reads DOB only); no `date_of_death` in crates/

**Verifier (tried to refute, could not).**

However, if your spouse died on February 12, 2025, your spouse isn't considered age 65 and wouldn't qualify for the enhanced deduction for seniors." Under parent D-10 tier 1 that is the STRONGEST class of evidence in this project ("a worked example in the instructions → a KAT with exact numbers … stronger than two oracles agreeing"), so filing it as an open question inverts D-10's own ranking. ### 3. Important — [recon-fidelity]

**Claim.** The spec's enumeration of the Part III input-definition traps ("Two definitional traps in the input surface, both from the recon") drops two of the recon's four exclusions from qualified overtime — that qualified overtime cannot include any amount received as a qualified tip (no double-dip with Part II), and that state-law-only overtime paid to FLSA-ineligible employees does not count at all.

**Scenario.** Tipped, FLSA-eligible employee, single, MAGI $120,000 (no phase-out): $8,000 of qualified tips on L4c, and a box-14 "FLSA Overtime Premium" figure of $2,000 of which $1,500 is premium computed on tip-inclusive pay already reported on L4c. With no prompt or cross-part validation built, L13 = $8,000 and L21 = $2,000 → L38 overstated by $1,500 while every test passes (both parts are individually under cap and under threshold, so no arithmetic guard fires). Same class for a filer whose only "overtime" is state-law overtime as an FLSA-exempt salaried employee: L21 is entirely fictitious.

**Fix.** Add both exclusions to §4.1's trap list (they are statutory, §225(d), and printed in the instructions) and require the Part III prompt to state them; add a green-suite case where the same dollars are offered to Part II and Part III and the built surface refuses or nets them out.

**Evidence.** design/ty2025/SPEC_schedule_1a.md:100-103 vs recon 03 lines 118-123 ("Excludes: ... state-law-only overtime for FLSA-ineligible employees, and **qualified tips** (no double-dip)"); i1040gi--2025 p.106: "• Qualified tips. Qualified overtime compensation doesn't include any amount you receive as a qualified tip." and "Overtime pay that is paid to these FLSA-ineligible employees is not qualified overtime compensation".

**Verifier (tried to refute, could not).**

CONFIRMED from both the archived primary source and the recon; nothing in the spec covers the gap.

1) EVIDENCE SAYS WHAT IS CLAIMED. `pdftotext -layout` of design/amt-form6251/i1040gi--2025.pdf, page 106, section "Amounts that are not qualified overtime compensation. The following amounts are not qualified overtime compensation and can't be included when figuring the deduction..." carries FOUR bullets: "Premium pay" (amount over time-and-a-half), "Payment for holidays and weekends" (without >40 hrs), "Qualified tips. No exclusion text appears anywhere in Part III, so faithful line-by-line transcription of the FORM (S-6 / the project transcription rule) does not carry these — they exist only in the instructions' narrative, which is exactly what §4.1's input-surface enumeration is for.

4) NO OTHER SPEC CLAUSE REACHES IT (whole spec read, 173 lines). S-1..S-5 and S-7 are rounding/MAGI/caps/Part-V/expiry. S-6's enumerated "see instructions" branches are 4a, 4c, 5, 22, 36a — 14a/14b are not in the list at all (the parent's version at SPEC.md said "At minimum:"; the child dropped that hedge and wrote "The branches, all answered in i1040gi--2025.pdf:"). §4.2 has no cross-part validation. §5's eight test items have no Part II/Part III interaction case and no FLSA-eligibility case. §6 R-2 restates only the same two traps ("Qualified overtime's premium-half rule and the SSTB exclusion"). §7's three open questions are Part V death, line 5 net-income limitation, Notice 2025-69. Parent SPEC.md:452's input surface says only "qualified overtime". Grep of design/ty2025/ for FLSA|ineligible|state.law|double.dip hits only the two spec lines at issue; parent reviews r1/r2 never raise it, so SETTLED does not rule it out.

## Refuted by verification

Recorded because a refuted finding still shows where reviewers look — and three of these were Criticals.

- **Critical** [constants] §4.1 defines the line-5 ceiling as "each business's net-profit ceiling", but the archived instructions define the net income limitation as net profit *reduced* by the deductions allocable to that business that are not on Schedule C/E/F — so building the spec as written overstates lines 5, 6, 7 and 13.
  - *Refuted:* REFUTED — the underlying tax fact is right, but every load-bearing part of the claim fails against the whole spec and the repo.

1. The evidence quote is accurate, and it is already the spec's own settled rule. `pdftotext -layout i1040gi--2025.pdf` line 7548 carries the quoted passage verbatim, and the general rule at 7486 ("...the gross income from the trade or business... minus the total of all deductions allocable to that trade or business, including the deductible part of self-employment tax; the deduction for contributions to self-employed SEP, SIMPLE, and qualified plans; and the self-employed health insurance deduction, but not including the deduction for qualified tips... Do not reduce it below zero"). But `design/full-return/recon/fable/03-followon-math-sch1a-qbi-ctc.md:95-100` — which SPEC_schedule_1a.md §1 makes binding ("already transcribes Schedule 1-A in full... Use it; do not re-derive") and §8 names "**the transcription of record**" — states it correctly and completely: `L5_contribution = min(qualified_tips_of_business, max(0, net_profit − allocable_deductions))` "where allocable deductions include the ½-SE-tax deduction (Sch 1 L15), self-employed SEP/SIMPLE and health-insurance deductions attributable to that business — but not the tips deduction itself. A net-loss business contributes $0." That is the archived rule, reduction included, with the exact Sch 1 L15 term the finding says is missing.

2. §4.1 is not where the spec defines the ceiling. It is headed "Inputs (~25 leaves)" and inventories collected leaves; the computation is defined in §4.2, which defines line 5 by transcription only ("transcribed line by line in the form's own numbering with the printed text as doc comments"), not by any formula. Nothing in the spec computes a ceiling as bare net profit.

3. A §2 BINDING decision already forces the reduction. S-6 lists "line 5's multi-business net-income limitation" among the "see instructions" branches that are "transcribed or refuses", sourced to `i1040gi--2025.pdf` — the very document cited. Transcribing that branch per the standing rule yields the Multiple Trades or Businesses Worksheet (extracted text, p.104-105), whose column (c) is "Other deductions allocable to the trade or business and not reported on Schedule C, Schedule E, or Schedule F", column (d) "Subtract column (c) from column (b)", column (j) "Enter the lesser of column (d) and column (i)", and line 2 "Add lines 1A through 1E, column (j), and enter the total on Schedule 1-A, line 5". The reduction arrives by construction, and it is the worksheet — not the form's bare line-5 text — that S-6 binds the build to.

4. The spec explicitly holds line 5 OPEN rather than settling it in §4.1's words. §7 OQ-2: "Per-business or aggregate for line 5's net-income limitation? The recon reads it per trade or business, with a net-loss business contributing $0. **Confirm against the instructions before coding.**" §4.1's phrase cannot be the operative definition of something the spec files as an unresolved question with a mandate to check the same instructions.

5. The SCENARIO cannot occur. There is no Schedule 1-A tips implementation and no test suite to be "green on the wrong ceiling": the only Schedule 1-A surface in the repo is the opaque scalar `schedule_1a_additional`, hardcoded `let schedule_1a_additional = Usd::ZERO;` at /scratch/code/bitcoin_tax/crates/btctax-core/src/tax/return_1040.rs:1225, plus `schedule_1a_l37` consumed by form6251.rs:275-332.
