## NEW findings

### I-new-1 — IMPORTANT — the reward-lot row is grouped **Covered** on a misreading of §1.6045-1(a)(15)(i)(J); the manual the spec already cites settles it the other way

**Where:** spec R1 table `:112` (`FmvAtIncome`, `CardRewardRebate` … | yes | **Covered** — (J) covers acquisition
"in exchange for … services", and the broker holds the acquisition record); T2 `:285-286` (the kill
`FmvAtIncome` in an exchange wallet dated 2026 → `Covered`); and the commit message ("group as Covered
per §1.6045-1(a)(15)(i)(J) 'or services'"). Sources: `legal/primary-sources/regulations-cfr/26CFR_1.6045-1_broker_reporting.xml`;
`legal/text/irs-forms/Instructions_1099-DA.txt:41-57`, `:105-113`, `:364-366`.

**What is wrong.** (J) in full reads:

> (J) A specified security described in paragraph (a)(14)(v) of this section that is acquired in a
> customer's account by a broker providing custodial services for such specified security on or after
> January 1, 2026, **in exchange for cash, stored-value cards, different digital assets, or any other
> property or services described in paragraph (a)(9)(ii)(B) or (C) of this section, respectively.**

The spec's own Legal-grounding quote `:49-50` elides that clause after "in exchange for cash …", and
r4 reaches through the ellipsis for the words "or services". The cross-reference decides it:
**(a)(9)(ii)(C)** is *"Dispositions of digital assets **in consideration for any services provided by
a broker** that is a real estate reporting person … or a broker that is in the business of effecting
sales of property"* — services the **broker renders and the customer pays for in digital assets**,
not services the customer performs. (a)(9)(ii)(B) is the parallel property limb, likewise bounded.
A staking/mining/interest credit or an FR-45 card rebate is not acquired *in exchange for* cash,
stored-value cards, a different digital asset, or (B)/(C) property or services. **(a)(15)(i)(K)** does
not rescue it either — it covers (a)(14)(vi) forward contracts, expressly "not described in paragraph
(a)(14)(v)". Under **(a)(16)** ("any specified security that is not a covered security") the lot is
**Noncovered**.

The IRS's own plain-language restatement, in a file this spec cites five times, says the same thing in
one sentence (`Instructions_1099-DA.txt:41-57`, right column):

> Covered security. For purposes of Form 1099-DA, a covered security is a digital asset acquired after
> 2025 **for cash, stored-value cards, different digital assets, or any property or services the
> disposition of which the broker is required to report** in an account for which the broker provided
> custodial services.

and `:364-366`: *"Rewards and staking payments. Do not report rewards and staking payments on Form
1099-DA."* Nothing in the reg mentions staking, airdrops or rebates; the one "reward" hit is the
loyalty-program disposition rule, which is not this.

**Why it blocks.** It does **not** produce a wrong box — (c) above proves the grouping cannot, because
the filer answers off the form. It blocks on two grounds the severity rule names directly:

1. **An unsound assumption stated as a legal determination.** The row is the one r3 forced a change on,
   and its replacement is a statutory reading the cited provision contradicts. Under this repo's
   transcribe-don't-paraphrase rule the table cell becomes a doc comment, and under the tax-authority
   hierarchy a misread reg must not decide a position. It is also the house recurring class: the answer
   was in the manual, already committed, already cited by this spec.
2. **It is the bucket that maximises spurious refusals.** The broker must report the *sale* of a reward
   lot (box 1f) but, being noncovered, will check box 9 and leave box 2 unchecked. In the **Covered**
   bucket that form sits beside 2026 purchases whose forms have box 2 checked → the key answers
   `Mixed` → **refuse**, and `Mixed`'s only exit is the per-lot import, which is out of scope. In the
   **Noncovered** bucket it sits beside pre-2026 lots and transfers-in, whose forms agree with it →
   `ProceedsOnly` → H/K, no refusal. So the correct citation and the better outcome point the same way.

**Minimal change:** move `FmvAtIncome`/`CardRewardRebate` in an exchange wallet to **Noncovered** for
**any** date, citing `Instructions_1099-DA.txt:41-57` and §1.6045-1(a)(16) (they may keep their own row —
the reason differs from the transfer row's); flip T2's kill `:285-286` to `→ Noncovered`; and spell out
(J)'s full "in exchange for" clause in the Legal-grounding bullet `:49-50` so no later reader reaches
through the ellipsis again. If the owner prefers to keep Covered as a *heuristic*, then say plainly
that it is a grouping heuristic and not a legal determination, and drop the (J) citation — but the
citation and the refusal rate both favour Noncovered.

Absent an adequate identification, §1.1012-1(j)(3)(i) determines the units sold as the **earliest acquired in that broker's custody** (transfer-in dates disregarded) — FIFO by regulation. The broker's own method governs only through (j)(3)(ii)'s last sentence, which deems a sole-method broker's method a standing order (and which Notice 2026-20 §4.03 switches off when §4.02 is used). Restate as: *without one, §1.1012-1(j)(3)(i) treats the earliest-acquired units in that broker's custody as sold — or, where the broker offers only one specific-ID method, (j)(3)(ii) deems that method a standing order — and btctax's HIFO row is wrong.*
