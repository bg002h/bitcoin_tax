## 3. FINDINGS

Ranked by how much each would matter to a real filer at a deadline.

**1. [Important — a real capability gap, cleanly refused] btctax cannot represent a non-crypto
brokerage 1099-B with "many small lots" at all — only an aggregate, no-adjustments total.**
What I wanted: enter the household's actual stock activity (mixed short/long lots, some basis not
reported, some with wash-sale adjustments — the ordinary shape of "heavy brokerage activity").
What happened, verbatim: `income import` refused outright (exit 2, nothing stored) —
`[Form1099BNeedsForm8949] the Form 1099-B from Meridian Discount Brokerage carries totals, but
Schedule D lines 1a and 8a accept totals ONLY for transactions "for which basis was reported to
the IRS and for which you have no adjustments" ... btctax fills Form 8949 from its own crypto lot
engine only, and will not report securities it cannot itemize`. Class: a **refusal** — well
worded, immediate (at import time, not buried at export time), names the exact remediation. What
I dropped: the "many small lots with adjustments" scenario; I substituted the IRS-legitimate
aggregate ("basis reported, no adjustments") path to keep the walk moving, which is real but a
materially less common real-world shape for an active trader. **This is not a bug** — the code
comments are explicit that this is a deliberate scope boundary (`btctax-core::return_inputs.rs`:
"which is exactly the lot-level engine btctax will not build for non-crypto securities") — but a
filer with genuinely messy stock activity (any wash sale, any non-covered lot, any broker that
doesn't report basis) has **no path through btctax for their stock disposals at all**, full stop,
and would need a separate preparer or tool for that half of their return. Worth being visible in
user-facing docs/help, not just a source comment, since "heavy brokerage activity" is a common
household shape.

**2. [Minor — labeling clarity, not a wrong figure] The "Federal tax attributable to crypto"
report section's "net short-term / net long-term" lines are WHOLE-RETURN totals, not
crypto-only, and the section header doesn't say so.** `report --tax-year 2024` printed `net
short-term: 3344.75   net long-term: 31679.85` directly under the heading "Federal tax
attributable to crypto — tax year 2024". The short-term figure happens to equal the crypto-only
total (no stock ST activity leaked in by coincidence of the netting), but the long-term figure
($31,679.85) is actually crypto LT gain ($24,779.85) **plus** the stock 1099-B's aggregate LT net
gain ($6,600) **plus** the 1099-DIV box 2a capital-gain distribution ($300) — confirmed by
checking `btctax-core::tax::types.rs`'s own doc comment on the field (`st_net`/`lt_net`: "§1222
within-character net short-term/long-term **WITH crypto**" — a *level* field, deliberately
whole-return, used as bracket-placement context for the *delta* figures printed below it, which
ARE correctly crypto-only). The delta figures (`TOTAL federal tax attributable to crypto (delta):
4452.82`) are correct; only the plain "net short-term/long-term" labels, sitting under a
crypto-only section header with nothing distinguishing "level" from "delta," could read to a
filer as "how much of my BTC's own gain was short vs. long," which they are not once the return
also carries non-crypto capital gains.

**3. [Minor — cosmetic, once you know why] Form 8949's four physical pages interleave
short-term/long-term rather than grouping them.** Page order was Part I (14 rows) → Part II (14
rows) → Part I again (8 rows) → Part II again (3 rows), rather than both short-term pages
together followed by both long-term pages. Functionally correct (every row present, every total
reconciles, nothing dropped) and probably follows some natural pairing/ordering in the emitter,
but a filer stapling by hand or flipping through the packet would find "Part I, Part II, Part I,
Part II" mildly disorienting compared to "Part I, Part I, Part II, Part II."

**Observed and correct (not findings, matching the task's own expected list, plus items this
household newly exercised):** no CTC/dependents (none claimed); standard deduction taken, no
itemizing; **the state refund correctly excluded from income** because `itemized_prior_year` was
answered "No" (Schedule 1 needed no Form at all — Form 1040 line 8 printed $0, and the packet
correctly shipped without a Schedule 1); **qualified dividends and long-term gain correctly
routed through the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet** (Schedule D line 20

ordinary-rate one); Schedule B Part III correctly triggered by ordinary dividends exceeding

## 5. WHAT SURPRISED YOU

- **The stock-1099-B refusal fired on the very first `income import`, not buried three commands
  later.** This is the single best-behaved refusal encountered in either walk: immediate, exit 2,
  nothing partially stored, and the error message names the exact TOML key and value to change.
  Compare to walk 1's Finding #1 (a crash) or Finding #3 (a silent wrong-shaped success) — this is
  what "fail loud and useful" looks like, even though the underlying limitation (no non-crypto
  lot engine, period) is real and somewhat surprising for a "tax app" in the general sense —
  it is very much still *Bitcoin* tax software first, and a filer coming in expecting full
  brokerage support would hit a wall on the first real 1099-B they tried to enter.
- **The default lot-selection method (HIFO) silently defeated my own test design.** I built 24
  buy/sell pairs assuming each sale would consume only its own paired lot (since I never held two
  lots simultaneously... or so I thought — I front-loaded all 12 "long-term" buys in 2022 before
  any of their sales happened in 2024, so by the time ANY of them sold, all 12 were sitting in the
  pool together). HIFO consumed the highest-cost lot available at each sale regardless of which
  "pair" I intended, scrambling my hand-assigned short/long labels and splitting several sells
  across 2-3 different acquisition dates. The AGGREGATE gain still matched exactly (proceeds minus
  basis is invariant to lot method), and the result is if anything a BETTER test of Form 8949
  (39 rows instead of a clean 24), but it is a reminder that "HIFO by default" is a real, load-
  bearing fact about how the tool behaves, not just a memory note — I had to `grep` and confirm it
  via `btctax config` mid-walk rather than assume FIFO.
- **`export-irs-pdf` just worked.** After reading walk 1's report going in, I expected to hit the
  same crash — this household is a completely different shape (single, no dependents, standard
  deduction, no mortgage) that exercises almost none of the same code paths, and it produced a
  clean 4-form packet on the first try. Good confirmation the earlier Critical really is fixed,
  at least along this axis.
- **A return can be fully correct with a Schedule 1 line answered and still ship with NO Schedule
  1 attached.** The state-refund gate ($650, correctly non-taxable) resolves to a genuine $0 on
  Schedule 1 line 1, and since nothing else on Schedule 1 applied either, the packet simply
  doesn't include the form — no blank Schedule 1, no placeholder. That's the right behavior, but
  it means "did the state-refund gate work?" isn't visible by inspecting the packet's form list;
  you have to check Form 1040 line 8 (which I did — it's $0).
- **The "Federal tax attributable to crypto" section quietly mixes in non-crypto capital gains**
  for its context lines while keeping its delta lines crypto-only (Finding #2) — a household with
  BOTH crypto and non-crypto capital gains is exactly the shape that exposes this, and walk 1
  (pure Bitcoin, no stock) never could have surfaced it.
