# Recon lens: AcroForm map porting, TY2025 → TY2026

**Headline: porting the maps is cheap and the existing gate catches most of it — but the one
form where a blind copy passes every committed check is Form 6251, and a blind copy there puts
the AMT itself on the wrong printed line. 39% of TY2024's field spellings did not survive into
TY2025; the fix for TY2026 is one derived test that already exists as an xtask subcommand.**

Everything below is a command run in this session against the prebuilt `./target/debug/xtask`.
No cargo, no make, no git, no network. Scratch working files are outside the repo.

### The known data point is CONFIRMED, exactly

Form 8995: 33 fields in both years, **15 spellings identical, 18 changed**, every one of the 33
matched to a widget within 2.0 pt of its TY2024 position — i.e. the printed form did not move and
the IRS renamed 18 of 33 anyway. The renames:

    f1_1→f1_01, f1_2→f1_02                                    (2, identity)
    Table[0].Ln1A_Row1[0]→Table[0].Row1i[0]  … Ln1E_Row5→Row1v (15, five table rows × 3 leaves)
    ReadOrderSubForm[0]→Line2_ReadOrder[0]                     (1)

`crates/btctax-forms/forms/2025/f8995.map.toml:6` now carries this number correctly
("**the IRS renamed 18 of the 33 fields for TY2025**"); the audit
(`design/agent-reports/2026-09-05-ty2025-map-AUDIT.md`, IMPORTANT 2) caught it at "21". My count
is independent of both and lands on 18.

### The renames have no direction, so no normalisation rule can absorb them

TY2025 zero-**padded** Form 8995 (`f1_3` → `f1_03`) and simultaneously zero-**unpadded** Form 8283
and Schedule D (`f1_01` → `f1_1`, `f2_04` → `f2_4`). Two forms, same year, opposite conventions.
There is no "normalise the spelling" shortcut; the names must be re-read from the PDF, per form,
every year.

`line11` is the AMT itself, and it prints in line 10's box. TY2025 added exactly **one** field to

wrong-number-not-a-crash class in its purest form: the map parses, the fill succeeds, the paper
looks right, the figure is on the wrong line.

## 3. Where a silent rename hides: the old spelling still exists, elsewhere

Of the 266 in-place renames, I classified each by whether the TY2024 spelling still exists anywhere
in the TY2025 PDF:

    form          renamed   old name STILL EXISTS (silent)   old name gone (loud)
    f1040              27                       11                       16
    f1040s3             6                        4                        2
    f1040sa             4                        0                        4
    f1040sb             2                        0                        2
    f1040sc             2                        1                        1
    f8283              18                        0                       18
    f8949             180                        4                      176
    f8995              18                        0                       18
    schedule_d          9                        3                        6
    TOTAL             266                       23                      243

**243 of 266 are loud** — the exists-check reds. **23 are silent**, and each one is a spelling that
survives the year but now names a box 12–252 pt away from where it used to be.

Worked example, Schedule C, pasted from `dump-fields`:

    2024:  475.2, 264.0- 576.0, 276.0  …Lines18-27[0].f1_39[0]
           475.2, 240.0- 576.0, 252.0  …Lines18-27[0].f1_40[0]
    2025:  475.2, 252.0- 576.0, 264.0  …Lines18-27[0].f1_40[0]
           475.2, 240.0- 576.0, 252.0  …Lines18-27[0].f1_39[0]

The two boxes swapped vertical order. The field names stayed pinned to their **content** and the
printed **letters** swapped: `crates/btctax-forms/forms/2024/f1040sc.map.toml:200` records
`f1_39 = line "27a"`; `crates/btctax-forms/forms/2025/f1040sc.map.toml:242` records the same field
as `line "27b"`. A copied map would carry the 2024 letter, both names exist, and nothing reds.
(Both cells are `rule = "unmodeled"` here, so no money moved this year — the mechanism is what
matters.)

★ Note the direction: keying a port off the **printed form** is what breaks on Schedule C; keying
off the **field name** is what survives. A geometry-only matcher gets this one wrong and must not
be the only witness — the label reader plus the map's own reasoning is what settles it.

## 4. Form 8949 is the expensive one, and it is the crypto form

TY2025 rewrote it around Form 1099-DA. From `design/forms/extract/f8949--2025.txt`:

    (A) Short-term transactions reported on Form(s) 1099-B showing basis was reported to the IRS
    (B) Short-term transactions reported on Form(s) 1099-B showing basis was not reported to the IRS
    (C) Short-term transactions, other than digital asset transactions, not reported to you on
        Form 1099-B or Form 1099-DA
    (G) Short-term transactions reported on Form(s) 1099-DA showing basis was reported to the IRS
    (H) Short-term transactions reported on Form(s) 1099-DA showing basis was not reported to the IRS
    (I) Short-term digital asset transactions not reported to you on Form 1099-DA or Form 1099-B

Three boxes became six, per part. Measured consequences:

    dump-fields, checkbox widgets:  2024 = c1_1[0..2] on 1,2,3   |  2025 = c1_1[0..5] on 1..6
    field count:                    244 → 202 (the three new boxes ate three data rows)
    map:  forms/2024/f8949.map.toml:3   rows_per_page = 14   table_token = "Table_Line1"
                                   :12  box_field = c1_1[2]  box_on = "3"      (Box C)
          forms/2025/f8949.map.toml:3   rows_per_page = 11   table_token = "Table_Line1_Part"
                                   :10  box_field = c1_1[5]  box_on = "6"      (Box I)

The crypto case moved from Box C to Box I, and TY2025's Box C now *explicitly excludes* digital
asset transactions. A port that kept `c1_1[2] / on="3"` would tick a box that says, in the IRS's
own words, "other than digital asset transactions" — on a return signed under 26 USC 6065. That is
one line of TOML and it is not derivable from any field-name diff; it required reading the form.

Also note `rows_per_page` 14 → 11 is a *pagination* change carried in the map, so 8949 overflow
behaviour is year-dependent, not just geometry.

**Forecast, flagged as a hypothesis and not a measurement:** the repo's own
`design/SPEC_lot_optimization_program.md:23` records that Notice 2026-20 extends relief only
through 2026-12-31 and that the permanent broker-communication rule starts 2027. The 1099-DA
regime is still moving, so Form 8949's box set is the single most likely thing to change again for
TY2026. Unknown until the form exists.

## 5. What the port actually costs, split three ways

For each of the nine ported forms I measured file composition (`wc -l`, `grep -c '^\s*#'`, distinct
quoted FQNs, `grep -c 'rule = '`):

    form           L2024   L2025   cmt24   cmt25   fqn24   fqn25  census25
    f1040s2          147     254      72     175      60      63        55
    f1040s3          105     165      53     115      39      37        30
    f1040sa          108     180      55     130      37      33         9
    f1040sb          168     231      51     115      72      72         2
    f1040sc          271     314     144     187     105     105        88
    f6251            164     225      94     154      61      62        18
    f8959             71     137      35     101      26      26         8
    f8960            100     140      49      89      38      38        22
    f8995            104     139      59      94      33      33        12
    f1040s1a (new)     —     221       —     151       —      54         —

    prose totals across the 9 ported forms:  2024 = 612 comment lines,  2025 = 1160

**(a) Mechanical, a tool does it, ~0 human cost.** The field inventory: `xtask dump-fields` on the
new PDF. Exact, free, and it is the entire answer for the 642-of-1055 spellings that survive.

**(b) Mechanically *checkable*, but a human must do the re-mapping.** When a name changes, the
exists-check tells you *that* it changed (243 of 266 cases) and the label reader tells you *where
the new box is*. Re-pointing 18 fields on Form 8995 is transcription, not judgment — but it is
transcription nobody can skip.

**(c) Not automatable, and it is where the whole cost sits.** Prose grew from 612 to 1160 comment
lines across the nine ports — **and it grew most on the forms where nothing changed at all**:
`f8959` had 26 identical fields, 0 renames, 0 new, 0 gone, and went 35 → 101 comment lines. That
is the provenance record the answered-ness rule demands (why each unmapped field is `unmodeled`,
what the sha256 is, which text-layer line the quoted instruction came from). A tool cannot decide
that btctax does not collect RRTA income.

**So: roughly 60% of the field surface is a free copy, ~15% is guided transcription, and the
irreducible cost is per-form prose that is nearly constant whether or not the form changed.**
The per-form floor is real: ~40–65 lines of new hand-written justification even for a zero-diff
form.

## 7. Two facts about the current batch that bear on the TY2026 port

Both are already logged in `design/agent-reports/2026-09-05-ty2025-map-AUDIT.md`; I re-measured
them because they change what "the maps are done" means.

- **10 of the 15 TY2025 map files are inert.** `crates/btctax-forms/src/map.rs` `include_str!`s
  only `f8949`, `schedule_d`, `schedule_se`, `f8283`, `f1040` for 2025 (5 of 15; 17 of 17 for
  2024). `map_pdf_conformance.rs` reaches them anyway because it walks the filesystem — but no
  compiled consumer loads them.
- **`f6251`'s TY2025 map cannot deserialize.** `crates/btctax-forms/src/map.rs:116` declares
  `pub line1: MoneyCell` (non-`Option`); `crates/btctax-forms/forms/2025/f6251.map.toml:72,75`
  declare `line1a` and `line1b`. `packet.rs:181` now dispatches by year via
  `Form6251Map::for_year(year)?`, and there is no `F6251_MAP_2025`, so TY2025 **refuses** —
  fail-closed, correct, and the reason the split has not bitten. There is also no `Schedule1AMap`
  struct anywhere, so the 54-field `f1040s1a` map has no deserialization target.

  For TY2026 this is the shape to expect again: *a struct field per form line* means any year that
  splits a line is a compile-time event, which is the good outcome — but only if the year's map is
  actually wired.

## CAN BE DONE TODAY (no IRS dependency)

1. **Promote the line↔label join into `map_pdf_conformance.rs` as a derived, filesystem-walk test.**
   It is the only check that catches the Form 6251 class, the instrument exists
   (`xtask label-boxes`), the fixtures exist (`design/forms/geometry/`, 12 files), and I have
   watched it go red on a planted blind copy (12/41) and green on the real batch (130 lines,
   0 mismatches) in this session. Pair it with that exact planted defect as its B1 kill-test.
2. **Add the y-descends-within-a-page check to the same walk.** It is currently per-form and
   year-pinned at `crates/btctax-forms/tests/f6251_map.rs:160`. Generalised, it needs no text layer
   and would have caught Schedule C's row swap.
3. **Write the port runbook as a script, not prose.** Every step I used is already a command:
   `dump-fields` both years → set-diff the name column → geometry-match at 2.0 pt → classify each
   rename as loud/silent → `label-boxes` join. Committing it as `scripts/` makes the year port a
   command instead of a discipline, and makes the silent class visible by construction.
4. **Fix `Form6251Map` to carry `line1a`/`line1b`** and give the TY2024 map both keys, so the
   TY2025 map has a deserialization target and TY2026 inherits a struct that already knows the
   line split. Add a `Schedule1AMap`.
5. **Carry `f1040s1`, `f8275`, `f8995a` to TY2025 now.** Their TY2025 PDFs exist (`f8275r--2025`
   is archived; per `design/forms/README.md` `f8275` is periodic-cadence and its TY2025 edition
   was byte-identical to TY2024). Doing them now means TY2026 is a one-year hop for all 18 rather
   than a two-year hop for three of them.
6. **Wire the ten inert TY2025 maps**, so the batch has a compiled consumer before another year is
   stacked on it.
