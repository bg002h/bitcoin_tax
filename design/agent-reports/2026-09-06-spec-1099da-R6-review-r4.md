## The dispatch as now written — the decision table an implementer would code

Three independent predicates, all reachable:

- **E** = `return_inputs::exists(conn, year)` — a **committed** row exists (`admin.rs:670`, unchanged).
- **A** = `broker_answers(conn, year)?` is `Some` and **non-empty** (T9, `:287-290`).
- **P** = `BundledFullReturnTables::load().full_return_for(year).is_some()` (`:283`).

R6's three arms: (1) `E ∧ P`; (2) `A ∧ ¬P`; (3) *"no answers stored"* = `¬A`.

| E | A | P | R6 arm | what the filer gets | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | (1) | full return, boxes from the **committed** row | matches — but see N-7 on when the accessor is called |
| T | F | T | (1) | full return | matches |
| **F** | **T** | **T** | **none** | falls to the `else` → arm (3) → the live-year refusal *"answer them in the TUI input form … then export again"* — which the filer already did | **M-13** |
| F | F | T | (3) | live year refuses; non-live fills | matches |
| T | T | F | (2) | slice, routed from the **draft** if one exists (C-4's fix) | matches |
| T | F | F | (3) | live year refuses naming the exit; non-live prints (the M-4 note applies) | matches |
| F | T | F | (2) | the modal R6 journey — slice from the draft | matches |
| F | F | F | (3) | as today | matches |

Seven of eight cells are determinate. The eighth (`¬E ∧ A ∧ P`) is not hypothetical: it is the modal
R6 journey — TY2026, TUI-authored answers, no committed row — on the day FR-47 bundles the TY2026
`FullReturnParams` (`forms/2026/YEAR.toml` says the record flips to `filable` *"in the same commit that
inserts the params"*). Nothing in R6 covers it, and the arm it falls into tells the filer to do the
thing they have already done.

Two further reads of the table, both worth stating because R6's own sentences invite them:

- **The precedence differs BETWEEN arms and R6 does not say so.** Arm (1) files from
  `return_inputs::get` (`admin.rs:1232`, committed only); arm (2) files from `load`
  (draft-shadows-committed). Same command, same vault, different rule — and which one applies flips by
  itself the day a build bundles the year's params. That is defensible design (the draft wins only where
  committing is *impossible*), but T9 states the opposite as an unconditional guarantee (`:386-387`),
  which is what makes I-15 a finding rather than a note: an implementer reading that sentence could as
  easily "fix" arm (1) to use the accessor, silently changing what the **full return** files.
- **Nothing in R6 forbids calling the accessor before arm (1) is ruled out** (N-7).
