### Why not a HARD gate either (this is the load-bearing constraint, and it is structural)

(b) offered *refuse or annotate*. **Refuse is unclearable, so it was ruled out on the merits, not on
test count.** `resolve::method_election_is_forward` is `me.effective_from >= TRANSITION_DATE &&
me.effective_from >= made` — an election **can never cover a past sale**, and `MethodElectionBackdated`
refuses one that tries. So a filer whose 2025 sales rest on the default has **no action that clears a
Hard blocker**: btctax would refuse their return permanently. The reg's own remedy (FIFO) is closed to
us by the mandate, and weakening the back-dating guard would gut what FR-33 just hardened.

The repo has already calibrated this exact question on the other side of the transition:
`Pre2025MethodNote` is **Advisory even when unattested**, for the identical "we assumed your lot
method" assumption in a period §1.1012-1(j) does not even reach. Making the post-2025 case Hard would
be stricter than the owner accepted for the same question, *and* unclearable.

`the_identification_advisory_never_gates_the_year` pins the severity **with that reasoning in the
test**, so a future reader meets the argument, not just the choice.

## 2. The authority — verified, not inherited

`legal/primary-sources/regulations-cfr/26CFR_1.1012-1_basis.xml`,
sha256 `909e34e1…cd81ba98`, **matches `legal/SHA256SUMS` line 21** (machine-checked, both sides
pasted). From the XML text layer:

- **(j)(1)** — "If a specific identification is not made, the basis and holding period of the units
  sold, disposed of, or transferred are determined by treating the units not held in the custody of a
  broker as sold … **in order of time from the earliest date** on which units … were acquired by the
  taxpayer."
- **(j)(3)(i)** — the same deemed acquisition order for broker-custodied units where "the taxpayer
  does not provide the broker with an adequate identification".
- **(j)(3)(ii)** — "**A standing order or instruction** for the specific identification of digital
  assets **is treated as an adequate identification** made at the time of sale, disposition, or
  transfer."

(j)(3)(ii) is what makes the fix's remedy real and one command wide, and it is why keeping HIFO is
lawful *once elected*: the mandate's own compliance clause said "the user is prompted to affirm it per
exchange. **Keep that surface**" — and that surface existed only for `pre2025_method_attested`, a
pre-2025-only flag. The post-2025 half had no surface and no disclosure. **That gap is FR-34.**

## 4. The fix

**HIFO is untouched.** `default_method_is_hifo` passes unmodified. What changed is that the verdict
now reads on the filing path.

1. **`compliance.rs` — `identification_made(date, selection_made, election_from) -> ComplianceStatus`.**
   The §A.5 classifier body, extracted. `disposal_compliance` now delegates to it (keeping the
   (j)(3) broker envelope as its own overlay), and **the fold calls the same function while it builds
   the basis**. One computation, so the verdict btctax reports and the verdict governing the number it
   files cannot drift.
2. **`fold.rs` — `applicable_method` returns `(LotMethod, Option<TaxDate>)`.** `None` ⇒ no election was
   in force ⇒ the method is btctax's, not the filer's.
3. **`fold.rs` — `consume_principal` takes an explicit `IdScope`.** `TaxableDisposition` at the three
   Dispose/GiftOut/Donate sites, `SelfTransferRouting` at the self-transfer site. Explicit so E0061
   forces a fifth call site to answer rather than acquire the exemption by omission — FR-33's
   `persist_selection` discipline.
4. **`pools.rs` — `peek_consumption(key, need, method)`.** Read-only counterfactual over a copy of the
   one pool, running the **same** `consume` the fold runs, so the advisory's numbers cannot drift from
   the real arithmetic.
5. **`state.rs` — `BlockerKind::IdentificationDefaulted`, Advisory.**
6. **`xtask/verdict_reach.rs`** — `ComplianceStatus`'s `ADVISORY_ONLY` entry **deleted** (not
   reworded), `UNREACHED_PIN` **5 → 4**, and `compliance_status_is_still_unread_on_the_filing_path`
   **inverted** to `compliance_status_now_reads_on_the_filing_path` — exactly what the pre-fix
   assertion's own failure message instructed. It now reds if a refactor severs the reader again.

### ★ It fires on EXPOSURE, not on formality

The advisory is raised only when the deemed acquisition order would take **different units** than the
default took. §1.1012-1(j)(1) prescribes an outcome, not paperwork: on a single-lot pool — the common
case — every method and the deemed order take the same unit, the return already *is* what the reg
prescribes, and there is nothing at stake. Firing there would hit nearly every disposal in an
un-elected vault and train the filer to ignore the one that matters.

**Direct evidence this scoping is right, not convenient:** four existing tests
(`evaluate_disposal_existing_no_selection_is_no_op_identity`,
`optimize_mode2 state_as_of_mixed_tz_straddle_disposal_not_skipped`,
`transition path_b_preserves_gift_tacking`, `transition path_b_preserves_gift_dual_loss_basis`) went
red under the unscoped version and green again under the scoped one **with no fixture edited**. Their
own message said why: *"the same basis the deemed order gives on this pool."*

### The comparison is over fragments, order-insensitively

Both halves were forced by mutation, and one was a live bug in my own first draft: FIFO returns
`[older, dearer]` where HIFO returns `[dearer, older]` whenever a sale drains a pool — the *same*
units, same bases, same terms, same tax. My initial pairwise `zip` fired an advisory there, on a
disposal with zero exposure. Now sorted-set. Killed by
`the_advisory_is_silent_when_the_deemed_order_takes_the_same_units_in_a_different_order`.

### The advisory names the money

> …btctax applied its **DEFAULT method HIFO**, consuming **$90.00** of lot basis.
> §1.1012-1(j)(1) / (j)(3)(i) treat units with no adequate identification as sold in order of
> acquisition (earliest first), which on this pool draws **$50.00**: the default takes **$40.00 MORE
> basis** than the deemed order, so this return reports **$40.00 LESS GAIN** than §1.1012-1(j) gives.
> A standing order IS an adequate identification (§1.1012-1(j)(3)(ii)): record one with
> `btctax config --set-forward-method <fifo|lifo|hifo>` … It cannot be back-dated, so for THIS
> disposition only a contemporaneous identification already in your own books supports HIFO.

Both figures are computed off the same pool. The remedy is named, and so is its limit.

**¹ and ² are the round's real findings, and both were survivors first.** M3 and M4 initially
SURVIVED with the whole suite green — because their fixtures held a *single* lot, so the exposure
suppression kept them silent no matter what the scope or date guard said. The scope guard and the

fragile). Both fixtures now hold two lots at different prices, and both mutations are killed by their
own named test. Without the mutation round, two of this fix's four guards would have shipped untested
while looking covered.

**M5 SURVIVED, and it is unkillable by construction — stated rather than papered over.** No test can
distinguish "compare fragments" from "compare Σbasis" while the default is HIFO: `hifo_cmp` breaks a
per-sat tie by acquisition date ASC, which is FIFO's own primary key, so equal totals imply the
identical set. The stricter predicate is kept because it is what the reg states ("the basis **and
holding period**") and it is what stops the check going silently wrong if the default ever moves off
HIFO — but **no guarantee is claimed for it**, and the code comment says exactly that. It is a latent
correctness property, not a tested one.

Related, same discipline: the `effect` string has **two** branches, not three. Under a HIFO default
only the first is reachable (HIFO is greedy on basis-per-sat, so it takes at least as much basis as
acquisition order can), so a third branch claiming "MORE gain" would be a sentence no test could ever
red. The fallback asserts no direction.

## 7. Open question for the owner — the one thing this fix does not do

**It does not recover the money.** A no-election disposal still files at HIFO and still reports less
gain than §1.1012-1(j) prescribes; FR-34 makes that visible and quantified instead of silent. The
reg's own remedy is FIFO, and applying it is a direct reversal of the `reconcile-defaults` mandate,
which I did not do.

The decision is now one line, with its cost measured: **flipping `fold.rs:49` to `LotMethod::Fifo`
reddens the nine tests named in §1 and nothing else.** The two live options are (i) leave the default
at HIFO with this disclosure, or (ii) rule that the mandate governs the *estimate* and the deemed
order governs the *filed* number — the mandate's own heading is "the auto-reconcile estimate is less
punitive", though its Change-1 text says "GLOBAL (real + auto-reconcile)". That reading is the owner's
to make, not mine.
