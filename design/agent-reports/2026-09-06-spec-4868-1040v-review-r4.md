## Verdicts (a)–(g)

### (a) N3-I1 — R1 binds Part I by name and lines 4–8 by number — **SOUND, and the justification holds with one recording gap.** ✅

**The join is safe by construction, not by argument.** Both counters that could pull a named key into
the line→label check reject it at the same place:

```rust
key.strip_prefix("line").is_some_and(|rest| rest.chars().next().is_some_and(|c| c.is_ascii_digit()))
```

`numbered_line_keys` (`label_reader.rs:1127-1128`) and `line_bindings`'s `numbered` closure
(`:1224-1231`) are the same predicate. None of `name`, `address`, `city`, `state`, `zip`, `ssn`,
`spouse_ssn` starts with `line`, so all seven are invisible to the gate — no mis-join is *possible*,
which is stronger than "does not occur today".

**The five numbered keys join correctly today, measured.** `f1_11`→"4", `f1_12`→"5", `f1_13`→"6",
`f1_14`→"7", `c1_1`→"8". `label_matches` is exact match with two narrow normalisations, so `"4"` vs
`"4"` … `"8"` vs `"8"` all pass and contribute **five** clean joins per year, with the label reader
untouched. This is the whole load-bearing claim of r4 and it is true.

**The stated reason for the caption treatment is the true reason.** Replayed: `f4868--2025` yields
candidates `[316.80, 371.91, 434.85, 576.00]` and Part I's `1`/`2` (x2 43.89) and `3` (x2 173.49) are
in buckets of 2 and 1. No mechanism that keeps or re-scores columns reaches them.

**The transcription rule.** `CLAUDE.md`'s amendment reads: *"fields are named for the line within a
transcription struct — the thing that says what one revision of one document prints, and is therefore
per line-set revision (`Form6251Map`, `Schedule1A`, the worksheets)."* `Form6251Map` is the
deserialized `.map.toml` (`crates/btctax-forms/src/map.rs`; `Schema::Form6251Map`,
`line_set.rs:241-242`), so `Form4868Map` is squarely a transcription struct and the amendment does
**not** exempt it. The rule's full sentence demands *both* halves — "named for the line … carrying the
official instruction text verbatim as its doc comment" — and r4 keeps the second, not the first.

**I nonetheless rule the deviation justified**, on the rule's own stated purpose (*"the dropped term
becomes invisible once the lines are gone"*):

- nothing is dropped — all three lines are bound, the printed number is in each doc comment, the
  `[census]` is total over all 17 boxes, and r1 audited the box→line assignment 32/32;
- the alternative is not neutral, it is **worse**: `line1`/`line2`/`line3` produces three *false*
  reds per year on `every_mapped_line_lands_on_its_own_printed_label` (`line1` landing on a box the
  reader labels "5"), and r3 measured the reader change that would fix that at 12 **wrong** Schedule B
  bindings;
- there is corpus precedent for named identity cells: `crates/btctax-forms/forms/2024/f1040.map.toml`
  binds `taxpayer_ssn`, `spouse_ssn`, `address_street`/`_city`/`_state`/`_zip`, and `Form1040Map`
  models them as a named `header: Option<Form1040HeaderCells>` rather than as lines.

What is missing is only the **recording**: the spec says *"the transcription rule is kept where it
lives"*, which reads as full compliance, where it is in fact a deliberate, measured deviation from the
naming clause. `FOLLOWUPS.md` §G-5 has a constellation-wide audit open against exactly this rule, and
an unrecorded deviation is the thing such an audit exists to find. → **R4-M2** (Minor).

### R4-I1 — Important — T5's reorder does not say where it sits, and the natural reading disables the geometry-fixture guard for all six declared grids

**Where:** T5, *"T5 reorders the walk: `numbered_line_keys` is counted first; when it is 0 AND
`GRID_MAPS` names `(year, form)`, the map goes to a third printed bucket … and the join is skipped"*
(`:214-217`), against the sentence two lines later, *"**Neither `max_unwitnessed` moves**"* (`:219`).

**What is wrong.** The walk has **two** `continue` arms above the join, not one
(`label_reader.rs:1594-1619`):

```rust
let stem = match &m.stem {                       // (1) the PDF-sha256 → geometry-fixture join
    Ok(s) => s.clone(),
    Err(why) => { r.unwitnessed.push(format!("{} — {why}", m.form)); continue; }
};
let join = match label_join(&stem) {             // (2) the label column
    Ok(j) => j,
    Err(why) => { r.unwitnessed.push(format!("{} — {stem}: {why}", m.form)); continue; }
};
```

r3 named arm (2) and T5 answers arm (2). Neither says anything about arm (1) — and "counted **first**"
reads most naturally as *first in the loop*, i.e. **above both**. Placed there, a declared grid never
reaches the sha256 join either, and the consequences are measured:

- **2025 loses a gate that can fail today.** `2025/f8949` and `2025/f8283` are declared grids with
  geometry. Delete `design/forms/geometry/f8949--2025.json` today and `every_map()` yields
  `Err("no geometry fixture was observed from …")`, 2025's `unwitnessed` goes 0 → 1, and
  `audit_year_reach` reds against `max_unwitnessed: 0`. After the reorder as most naturally read, that
  map is diverted to the grid bucket **before** the fixture is ever looked for, and the same deletion
  is silent. The ratchet does not move; it stops being able to fire. That is the "gate that cannot
  fail" class, and the spec's *"Neither `max_unwitnessed` moves"* is the sentence that makes it look
  safe.
- **2017's consumed allowance drops 5 → 3.** All five TY2017 maps are unwitnessed today (no archived
  authorities), and two of them (`f8949`, `f8283`) are declared grids. Diverting them frees two slots
  under `max_unwitnessed: 5` for a genuinely unreachable map to land in silently, and makes the floor's
  own `why` (*"None of its five forms is archived"*) describe three. `2017/f8949`'s `GRID_MAPS` reason
  also says nothing about geometry, so it would be reported as *"grid — positional transaction rows"*
  when the reason it is not checked is that no fixture exists — the two blanks looking alike again,
  in the one clause written to stop that.

Placed **between** arm (1) and arm (2), none of this happens: a declared grid that loses its fixture is
still `unwitnessed`, and only the label-column refusal is bypassed — which is the only thing r3's
finding was ever about.

**Minimal change:** one clause in T5 — *"the grid branch sits AFTER the `m.stem` geometry join and
BEFORE `label_join`, so a declared grid that loses its geometry fixture is still counted unwitnessed"*
— and one more kill beside the one already named: *deleting a declared grid's geometry fixture must
still red its year.* (That kill is also the cheapest possible B1 demonstration, since the six grid maps
are the only population it applies to.)
