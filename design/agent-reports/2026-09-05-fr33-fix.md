# FR-33 — a post-hoc `LotSelection` was applied to the reported basis with no made-date guard

**Branch:** `fix/fr33-posthoc-lot-selection` (from `feat/schedule-1a-ty2025` @ `2e75b73d`)
**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Gate:** `make check` — **2787 passed / 12 skipped / 0 failed**, clippy `-D warnings` clean, `cargo fmt --check` clean.

---

## 1. The defect, reproduced before it was fixed

A `LotSelection` recorded **after** the sale was applied to the reported basis unconditionally. The
test written first (`crates/btctax-core/tests/lot_selection.rs::post_hoc_selection_is_ignored_and_the_disposal_falls_back_to_method_order`)
put a forward FIFO standing order in force, sold on 2025-07-01 for $95, and then typed a lot selection
on **2026-04-01** naming the high-basis lot:

```
thread 'post_hoc_selection_is_ignored_and_the_disposal_falls_back_to_method_order' panicked
  left: 90.00
 right: 50.00
```

Reported basis **$90** against the in-force order's **$50** — gain **$5** where the identification in
force gives **$45**. A $40 understatement on one $95 sale, from one keystroke sequence, with nothing
in the projection objecting.

---

## 2. The authority — what is archived, and what I relied on

**Archived and used:** `legal/primary-sources/regulations-cfr/26CFR_1.1012-1_basis.xml`.
SHA256 `909e34e1…cd81ba98`, and that hash **matches `legal/SHA256SUMS` line 21** — provenance verified,
not assumed.

★ **The 2026-09-05 audit's caveat was wrong on this point.** It warned that §1.1012-1(j) "may NOT be"
in `legal/primary-sources/`. It is: the whole of §1.1012-1 including paragraph (j) is there, as is
`irs-guidance/RevProc_2024-28.pdf`. Nothing in this fix rests on an unarchived source, and nothing
rests on Rev. Proc. 2024-28 (I did not need it — it governs the safe-harbor allocation, not
identification timeliness).

Three sentences do all the work. Extracted from the XML text layer, verbatim:

**§1.1012-1(j)(2) — the requirement.** "A specific identification of the units of a digital asset sold,
disposed of, or transferred is made if, **no later than the date and time of the sale, disposition, or
transfer**, the taxpayer identifies on its books and records the particular units to be sold, disposed
of, or transferred by reference to any identifier … sufficient to identify the units sold, disposed of,
or transferred."

**§1.1012-1(j)(1) — the consequence, for assets NOT in a broker's custody** (btctax's whole domain).
"**If a specific identification is not made**, the basis and holding period of the units sold, disposed
of, or transferred are determined by treating the units not held in the custody of a broker as sold,
disposed of, or transferred **in order of time from the earliest date on which units of the same digital
asset** not held in the custody of a broker **were acquired by the taxpayer**."

**§1.1012-1(j)(6) — applicability.** "This paragraph (j) is applicable to all acquisitions and
dispositions of digital assets **on or after January 1, 2025**."

So the reg does not say "refuse". It says a late identification **is not an identification**, and then
supplies the fallback itself.

The audit's claim that this consequence "exists in three doc comments and no code" was accurate. It is
now in code.

## 3. What I chose, and what I rejected

**Chosen: (b) — apply the reg's own consequence — plus (c)'s attestation key.**

An unattested `LotSelection` whose made-date is after the tax-date of the taxable disposition it names
is **dropped**, the disposal consumes by the method in force, and an **Advisory** `LotSelectionPostHoc`
blocker discloses it. The year still computes.

**Why (b) and not (a) refusal.** This repo transcribes a rule rather than inventing one, and here the
rule states its own remedy. Three further reasons made it not a close call:

1. **A refusal buys nothing.** The filer's escape from a refusal is `reconcile void` on the selection —
   which lands them at exactly the same method-order number. A refusal would be pure friction in front
   of an outcome the reg already mandates.
2. **The direction of error is safe.** Dropping a cherry-pick can only **raise** the reported gain or
   leave it unchanged. It is structurally incapable of the understatement FR-33 describes.
3. **Refusal is the wrong instrument for a fact about the past.** Nothing the filer does today can make
   a 2025 identification timely. Gating the return on an unfixable condition is a dead end, not a gate.

**But (b) alone silently changes a filed number, so the advisory carries the whole disclosure burden** —
and it is not a "figure with no reader". `render_verify` prints `Advisory blockers:` and then every
blocker's full `detail` (`crates/btctax-cli/src/render.rs:2769-2777`), and the TUI Compliance tab does
the same (`crates/btctax-tui/src/tabs/compliance.rs:134`). The detail names both dates, both reg
paragraphs, the direction of the change, and **both** remedies. That is asserted, on the rendered text,
by `verify_surfaces_the_post_hoc_selection_advisory_and_does_not_gate`.

**Why (c)'s key was mandatory, not optional.** `optimize accept` with a narrow per-disposal `--attest`
persists a selection whose made-date is after the sale **by construction** — that is precisely what
`Persistability::NeedsAttestation` means. A guard keyed only on the date would have made the
owner-sanctioned §C.2 path **inert**: the event would persist, the UI would report success, and the
projection would quietly ignore it. So the guard is keyed on the attestation.

**Rejected — reading the attestation from the `optimize_attestation` side-table.** This is the obvious
cheap route and it is wrong twice over:

- §7.1 makes the projection a **pure function of the event log**. A filed number keyed to a CLI-side
  sqlite table is not that.
- The TUI **deliberately never loads it** — `btctax-tui/src/unlock.rs` [R0-M3], stated twice in comments.
  So `btctax report` and the TUI would print **different basis figures for the same vault**.

The attestation therefore rides the ledger event: `LotSelection { .., attested: bool }`, `#[serde(default)]`
(⇒ pre-existing records read `false`, the fail-closed direction). The side-table keeps what it is
uniquely for — the attestation **text**, which binds the exact picks (R2-I1).

**Rejected — extending the guard to `SelfTransfer`.** §1.1012-1(j)(1)/(j)(2) do reach "transfers", and
Example 2 at (j)(5)(ii) is a transfer. But the guard is scoped to the taxable dispositions
(`Dispose`/`GiftOut`/`Donate`), matching the boundary `disposal_compliance` already draws and documents.
Widening it would change lot **routing** between the filer's own wallets, whose downstream effect on
later per-wallet HIFO is not monotone — it could raise or lower a later gain. That is a distinct
behavioural change deserving its own cycle, not something to smuggle into a Critical fix. **Recorded as
an open question in §7, not silently resolved.**

## 4. What changed

**The guard** — `crates/btctax-core/src/project/resolve.rs`, §A.4 block. A `selections.retain` that drops
an unattested selection whose made-date exceeds the deadline, keyed by `identification_deadline`
(taxable dispositions with tax-date ≥ `TRANSITION_DATE`, per (j)(6)).

★ **Order is load-bearing, and getting it wrong was caught by the suite, not by review.** My first
placement put the timeliness check *inside* the collection loop, i.e. **before** the targeting and
principal-conservation validation. `make check` immediately red:

```
verify_partitions_lot_selection_invalid_as_hard
  LotSelectionInvalid must appear in report.hard; hard blockers: []
```

An **Advisory had pre-empted a Hard**: a selection that was both malformed and late stopped gating the
year. The guard now runs **last** among the §A.4 filters, and the invariant is written down at the call
site. Residual, stated in the comment: a late selection that would *additionally* fail a **fold**-level
check (existence / per-wallet / over-draw) surfaces only the advisory, because the fold never sees a
dropped selection — already true of every resolve-level rejection, and the number is identical either way.

**The producers**, all four, exhaustively enumerated by `E0063` rather than by grep:

| producer | stamps | why |
|---|---|---|
| `cmd::reconcile::select_lots` | `false` | collects no attestation |
| `cmd::reconcile::import_lots` (CSV) | `false` | ditto |
| `cmd::optimize::persist_selection` | caller's `bool`, **required parameter** | `true` only on the `NeedsAttestation` branch |
| `tui_edit::persist_optimize_accept` | `attestation.is_some()` | derived from the same value the side-table row is written from, so they cannot disagree |

`persist_selection` takes `attested` as a **required parameter, not a defaulted field**, so a future
fifth call site cannot acquire the exemption by omission.

**Not changed, deliberately:** `disposal_compliance`. It still reports an unattested post-hoc selection
as `NonCompliant`. One could argue it should now fall through to `StandingOrder` (the standing order is
what actually governed after the drop) — but that flips a red row green, and the existing hardened KAT
`post_hoc_selection_with_in_force_election_is_noncompliant` was written specifically to stop that
labelling. Conservative direction, no gate weakened, hardened test left standing. Noted in §7.

## 7. Open, and explicitly not done

- **`SelfTransfer` routing** (§3). (j)(1)/(j)(2) reach "transfers"; the guard does not. Distinct
  behavioural change, non-monotone effect on later gains, deserves its own cycle.
- **FR-34 is untouched and still open**, and it bounds what this fix achieves: after the drop, a
  no-election disposal falls back to **HIFO** (`fold.rs:49`), where §1.1012-1(j)(1) says acquisition
  order — FIFO. FR-33 removes the *cherry-pick*; FR-34 owns the *default*. Fixing the default here
  would have been scope creep into another item's territory.
- **FR-35** — the comparison is date-granular; the reg says "date **and time**". Cited in the boundary
  test's doc comment so the next reader finds it.
- **`disposal_compliance` coherence** (§4) — arguable, deliberately left.
- `FOLLOWUPS.md` is **not** edited, following the sibling FR-32 fix's precedent on this branch and
  keeping the diff clear of the FR-31 branch.
