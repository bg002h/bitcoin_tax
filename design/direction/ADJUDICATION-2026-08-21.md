## D3 — P1 adverse branch

**ANSWER: REFUSE** (the plan's recommendation stands, on corrected grounds). The implementer builds S1 verbatim: `ScheduleAInputs::mortgage_within_debt_limit: Option<bool>` (prompt phrased so the deductible answer is affirmative), `RefuseReason::MortgageDebtLimitUnanswered` on `None` (not in question), and `RefuseReason::MortgageOverDebtLimit` on the adverse answer — written like `AmtNonQualifiedDwelling`, with a message that (1) states both failure directions: deducting the full 1098 amount would UNDERSTATE tax; zeroing 8a would OVERSTATE it by the whole deductible portion; (2) names the cure the form itself prescribes: Pub. 936's Deductible Home Mortgage Interest Worksheet; (3) notes the result of that worksheet will become enterable via the already-filed `mortgage_interest_deductible` input (FOLLOWUPS P9(a)/S2), which is the branch's designed endgame. B1 kill-tests per branch.

**AUTHORITY:** i1040sca (2024), Line 8a: *"If your home mortgage interest deduction is limited, see Pub. 936 to figure the amount of mortgage interest and points reported to you on Form 1098 that are deductible. **Only enter on line 8a the deductible** mortgage interest"* — the form's output for this filer is a determinate **nonzero** worksheet result. A printed $0 is not the transcription of any instruction; it is software substituting a number the form does not produce (the exact compression the transcribe-don't-paraphrase rule bans). Second, the asymmetry the plan leaned on is real but needs restating precisely: the line-8 checkbox is the **mixed-use disclosure** ("Check the box on line 8 if … you didn't use all of your home mortgage proceeds…"), which i1040sca makes mandatory in that situation — so the house's existing mixed-use-zero (`mixed_use_mortgage_forgone`, tested at `return_1040.rs:3506`) is a *disclosed* conservative zero, exactly like the zero-basis floor that rides Form 8275. Every conservative substitute figure this house has ever shipped carries an on-packet disclosure instrument. Schedule A provides **no** instrument for the debt limit, so an over-limit $0 would be the first undisclosed one. Third, the plan's own ordering principle: a wrong-direction figure signed silently under §6065 (a stderr advisory is not on the packet — the Section-B-no-appraiser precedent already adjudicated stderr-with-exit-0 as effectively silent) outranks in badness a loud refusal that produces no paper.

So: is an unexplained $0 on 8a worse than no return at all? **Yes, for this filer** — it silently overpays five figures (the [RAN] vector forgoes ~$49k of deductible interest, ~$18k of tax), can flip the auto itemize/standard election, embeds itself in every figure below 1040 line 12, and the packet cannot explain itself. The refusal is loud, answerable, and hands the filer the exact worksheet the instructions prescribe.

**ADVERSE BRANCH ACCEPTED:** Filers with combined acquisition debt over $750,000 ($375k MFS / $1M pre-2018) cannot export a full return from btctax until the S2 collection input lands. Acceptable: it is a top-of-axis minority, btctax has no installed base, `report` still runs, and the refusal message contains the complete manual path.

**WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WRONG:** (a) an on-form disclosure mechanism for the limit — I verified the TY2024 Schedule A extract has none; (b) an owner ruling that fileability-with-undisclosed-conservative-zero outranks the disclosed-conservatism pattern (the owner has blessed conservative zeroes, but always disclosed); (c) S2 shipping in the same cycle — then collect `mortgage_interest_deductible` directly and refuse only when *that* is also unanswered, which is strictly better and consistent with this ruling.

## D4 — P4 scoping

**ANSWER: REFUSE on `None`** (the plan's recommendation stands), with the liveness scoping made part of the decision, not an ornament. The implementer builds: `Option<bool>` mirroring `donations_had_restrictions` (`decl_tristate!`), LIVE only when (i) the return **itemizes** and (ii) any **single** contribution is ≥ $250 (per-contribution — never the year aggregate). `None` while live → refuse; `Some(false)` → refuse; `Some(true)` → proceed. The refusal message must carry the cure and its deadline verbatim from i1040sca: the acknowledgment is contemporaneous if obtained *"by the date you file your return or the due date (including extensions), whichever is earlier"* — i.e., **the filer can still get one, right up until they file; don't attach it, keep it**. The line-text restoration ships regardless.

**AUTHORITY:** §170(f)(8)(A): *"No deduction shall be allowed … for any contribution of $250 or more unless the taxpayer substantiates the contribution by a contemporaneous written acknowledgment"* — a strict statutory precondition of allowability, not a recordkeeping nicety. §170(f)(8)(C): contemporaneous = obtained by the **earlier of** filing or the due date. That earlier-of rule is what decides this: filing is the point of no return — a filer who exports and files without a CWA has permanently extinguished the cure (the post-filing acknowledgment fails (C) by its terms; *Durden v. Comm'r*, T.C. Memo. 2012-140, is the standing illustration). Now the repo's own three-part test, applied to proceeding-on-`None`: (1) **wrong number** — if the CWA doesn't exist, the claimed deduction is disallowed by law and tax is understated; (2) **fabricated testimony** — Schedule A lines 11/12 print "*If you made any gift of $250 or more, see instructions*" on the form's face, and the instructions' first sentence is the CWA rule; claiming the deduction unasked answers that liveness predicate *for* the filer — the answered-ness class exactly; (3) **silently lost right** — export-time is the only moment software can protect the still-curable right, and this repo has already adjudicated stderr advisories as non-gates. It fails all three prongs, so it is a refusal, not a skippable item. The owner's "too aggressive with refusals" correction is honored **structurally**, by liveness: a standard-deduction filer, or one whose every gift is under $250, is never asked — silence forgoes nothing and asserts nothing, which is the `donations_had_restrictions` pattern already codified at `return_1040.rs:2151`.

The sharp line that keeps this from generalizing into gate-everything: **refuse where filing itself extinguishes the cure; advise where the record can be assembled later.** This is why the CWA gates but §170(f)(17) bank-record substantiation for small cash gifts must NOT be turned into a gate by analogy — those records have no filing-linked deadline.

**ADVERSE BRANCH ACCEPTED:** Every itemizer with a ≥$250 single gift answers one extra tristate question, including the large majority who hold a CWA — friction with no numeric change for them. Acceptable: one form-posed question, answerable immediately, against a fail-open alternative that signs a legally unverifiable claim; and btctax has no users yet to disrupt.

**WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WRONG:** an owner product ruling that substantiation possession is out of software's domain wholesale (that ruling would have to unwind `donations_had_restrictions` too — they stand or fall together); or evidence that the liveness predicate misfires (e.g. gating a filer whose ≥$250 gift is excluded from the claimed deduction entirely).

## ERRATUM (added 2026-08-22, after the phase-2 review)

**D4's parenthetical mislabels its own referent.** The ruling says the input should mirror
`donations_had_restrictions` "(`decl_tristate!`)" — but `donations_had_restrictions` is a
**skippable**, built with `skippable_tristate!`. The two halves of that sentence contradict each
other, and they are not interchangeable: `decl_tristate!` is always-live, so building it would have
refused every standard-deduction filer at the registry loop — precisely what D4's operative text
forbids ("a standard-deduction filer … is never asked", honored "structurally, by liveness").

The implementer followed the ruling's REASONING over its LABEL and built the skippable. The phase-2
reviewer independently ruled that correct and this text wrong. Recorded here rather than silently
edited, because the ruling is the artifact under review and the correction is part of its history.

★ Nothing else in D4 changes; the behaviour it mandates is exactly what shipped.

## D5 — Form 8960 line 9b

**ANSWER: YES — line 9b is capped by §164(b)(6).** The sources settle it; no conservative-branch fallback is needed (though the settled answer *is* the conservative branch). Since decision 6 (collect-or-blank) already stands, the cap lands as a **validation bound on the collected value**, not a computation: reject a collected 9b exceeding the state/local income tax **actually deducted on the return after the cap** — on btctax's surface, min(the income-tax amount in line 5a, line 5e, $10,000/$5,000 MFS); if the filer elected general sales taxes on 5a, the bound is $0 (sales taxes are never deductible for NII — i8960 says so expressly). The prompt/advisory text states the allocable pool is the capped deducted amount and names i8960's own example (line 8 ÷ AGI ratio) as a reasonable allocation method applied to that pool.

**AUTHORITY:** §1411(c)(1)(B) (archived at `legal/primary-sources/statute-irc/26USC_s1411.html`): net investment income is reduced only by *"the deductions **allowed by this subtitle** which are properly allocable to such gross income or net gain."* §164(b)(6)(B) (TY2024 text): the aggregate SALT *"taken into account … shall not exceed $10,000 ($5,000 …)"*. State income tax paid above the cap is therefore **not a deduction allowed by subtitle A at all**, so there is nothing for §1411(c)(1)(B) to allocate — the statute alone closes this. The regulation agrees in structure: §1.1411-4(f)(1)(i) admits *only* deductions described in (f); **(f)(3)(iii)** — note: the brief's cite of (f)(3)(vi) is off; (vi) is §212(3) amounts — admits *"State, local, and foreign income … taxes described in section 164(a)(3)"*, i.e. the chapter-1 **deduction**, not taxes paid; and (f)(7) states the flow-through principle for the chapter-1 limits that existed when the reg was written (2013): deductions count *"only to the extent the items are deductible for chapter 1 purposes after the application of"* the limitation. §164(b)(6) postdates the reg, which is why it is unnamed — that is silence, not disagreement: **no statute/reg conflict exists and no AUTHORITY_CONFLICTS.md entry is warranted.** The instructions' two words are not a deliberate drafting distinction but staleness: the 9b sentence ("taxes you **paid** … attributable to net investment income") is pre-TCJA boilerplate, while the same instructions' allocation block defines the allocable item as SALT *"if **properly deducted on your return** when calculating your U.S. regular income tax"* and the update note still keys to §68 (suspended) — the document read whole supports capped, and it is not law anyway. TY2024 cap is $10,000/$5,000 (the 2025 "applicable limitation amount" amendment is effective for years after 2024 and is out of scope).

**ADVERSE BRANCH ACCEPTED:** ~$3,600 more NIIT on the plan's vector than the uncapped reading would produce, and a filer with a genuinely aggressive uncapped position cannot express it in btctax. Acceptable: the capped figure cannot understate tax, the uncapped position contradicts the statute's plain "allowed by this subtitle," and a filer taking it would need Form 8275-class disclosure btctax should not manufacture.

**WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WRONG:** IRS guidance published in the IRB (the instructions' own "supplemental guidance" clause) or an amended §1.1411-4 expressly allowing pre-cap "paid" amounts; or a court holding "properly allocable" operates on amounts paid rather than allowed. Also a scope caveat: **foreign** income taxes are excluded from the cap by §164(b)(6)'s flush sentence — irrelevant while btctax models none, but the bound must be revisited if that ever changes.

## D7 — §170(f)(11)(D) threshold

**ANSWER: CLAIMED — precisely defined.** The predicate operand is **the deduction claimed for the property**: post-§170(e) reduction, aggregated across all similar items donated in the year (all crypto donations aggregate), and determined **without regard to the §170(b) AGI ceiling / §170(d) carryover split** — i.e., the Form 8283-level claim btctax already computes as `claimed_deduction`, *not* the ceiling-limited Schedule A line 12 figure and *not* raw FMV contributed. The implementer keys the $500,000 attach-the-appraisal gate to that pre-ceiling claimed amount. On the brief's generalization vector ($700k gift, $1M AGI, year-1 Schedule A $300k): the gate **fires** — claimed-for-the-item is $700k. Conversely, ST-held crypto with FMV $700k but a §170(e) basis-limited claim of $180k does **not** fire it. One disclosure follow-up (owned by P6): the attach obligation recurs on every carryover-year return, so the carryover-out advisory should say so in one sentence.

**AUTHORITY:** §170(f)(11)(D) operative text (archived `26USC_s170.html`): *"In the case of contributions of property for which **a deduction of more than $500,000 is claimed**, the requirements … are met … if [the taxpayer] attaches to the return for the taxable year a qualified appraisal."* The word is *claimed*; the "contributed" lean comes only from the subparagraph **heading** ("Substantiation for contributions of more than $500,000"), and §7806(b) denies headings and descriptive matter any legal effect — so the instructions and the statute do not actually diverge here, and I am following the statute's operative sentence, which the instructions happen to match. What "claimed" means is fixed by the statute's own structure plus the reg: §170(f)(11)(F) makes the thresholds **property-level** ("property and all similar items … treated as 1 property"); Reg. §1.170A-16(f)(5)(ii) aggregates "the amount claimed as a deduction for all similar items … contributed during the taxable year"; and Reg. §1.170A-16(f)(3) extends the attach duty to *"the return for any carryover year under section 170(d)"* — a rule that is only coherent if the trigger follows the claim for the contribution across years rather than the annual post-ceiling slice. A per-year post-ceiling reading would also make substantiation depend on AGI (the $700k gift would *never* require attachment for a $1M-AGI filer while requiring it for a $3M-AGI filer claiming the identical property) — absurd against the provision's valuation-policing purpose, and contradicted by Form 8283's architecture, which reports the full claimed value in the contribution year while §170(b) operates downstream on Schedule A.

**ADVERSE BRANCH ACCEPTED:** btctax will demand an attached appraisal from some filers whose current-year Schedule A deduction is under $500,000 (ceiling-limited claims) — extra paper the strict per-year reading might excuse. Acceptable: the attachment is at worst surplusage and at best exactly what §1.170A-16(f)(3) requires anyway; the opposite error (predicate wired to line 12) silently drops a statutory attachment for the largest claims in the product.

**WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WRONG:** authority holding "claimed" means the year's ceiling-limited allowed amount — none found, and Reg. §1.170A-16(f)(3)/(f)(5) point the other way; or a misreading on my part of §170(e)'s interaction (if the owner wants maximal conservatism, keying to max(claimed, FMV) can never under-fire, but it is not what the statute says).
